01 · Brief

"By ten, half the shelf is empty."

Goals

  1. Let regulars reserve today's bake before it sells out.
  2. Turn cake DMs into one complete request.
  3. Make allergens findable without asking at the counter.
  4. Show when each bake comes out.

Constraints

  • 4-person team
  • Baking starts at 4am
  • Existing point-of-sale system
  • No staff time for admin
  • One phase

02 · Research

The shelf empties on a schedule.

Illustrative data: figures show the research method.

  • Sell-out analysisSix weeks of point-of-sale data: when does each item run out?
  • Counter observationTwo mornings, 6:45–11am, logging every question asked
  • Cake DM audit40 Instagram cake-order threads, from first message to confirmation
  • 10 intercept interviewsFive minutes at the counter, with a free coffee as thanks
14messages, on average, to confirm one cake order
2.3 daysfrom first message to a confirmed cake
1 in 5cake threads included an allergen question
31"What's left?" or "When is it out?" questions in two mornings

Key insights

  1. Regulars plan, but can't commit. They know what they want, just not whether it'll still be there.I'd happily pay last night if it meant the bun was mine.Intercept interview 4
  2. Cake orders stall on two facts. Price and the earliest date caused most of the back-and-forth.I just wanted to know if Saturday was possible.DM audit, thread 17
  3. Allergens are asked out loud, every day. Staff answer the same questions while the queue grows.Does this one have nuts? What about this one?Counter observation, day 2
  4. Timing is local knowledge. Regulars know the rye comes out at 9:30. Newcomers arrive at 11 and find nothing.

Key decision: reservations print in the kitchen at 6am, so bakers can bake more of what's pre-ordered rather than set aside existing stock. That turns pre-orders into extra sales, not just redistributed ones.

07 · Usability testing

Five customers, four tasks, five fixes.

Illustrative results: a first-click test followed by moderated sessions on participants' phones.

Task success, round 1 and round 2
TaskRound 1Round 2
Find a pastry safe for a nut allergy2/55/5
Reserve 2 croissants for tomorrow at 8am4/55/5
Price a birthday cake for 15 people3/55/5
Find out when sourdough comes out5/55/5

  1. A "Show nut-free" filter implied a guarantee, and three people distrusted it.

    Now it's "Hide anything containing…", plus an honest note about traces.

  2. Sold-out items disappeared, so people thought the bakery didn't make them.

    Now they stay visible, with "Sold out today · Pre-order for tomorrow".

  3. The cake price only appeared at the end.

    Now a live price sits under the preview, with an itemised summary.

  4. The date picker allowed tomorrow and Mondays, and the request bounced.

    Now the earliest date is shown up front, and impossible dates explain why.

  5. The pickup code was small text in a sentence.

    Now it's a large, dashed "ticket" that staff can read at arm's length.

08 · Handoff

Runs itself while the team bakes.

For the developer

  • Stock sync from the point-of-sale system every 5 minutes, with a "last updated" timestamp
  • Business rules as config: same-day cut-off, 3-day cake lead time, closed days
  • Component states: in stock, low, sold out, reserved, error
  • Analytics events: reserve, preorder_tomorrow, allergen_filter, cake_request

For the team

  • 6am kitchen print-out of the day's reservations
  • A "Reserved" shelf by the door, sorted by pickup time
  • Cake requests arrive as one structured email, not a DM thread
  • Unclaimed orders go to the food bank at closing

Phase 2 roadmap

  1. Pay aheadOptional card payment to cut no-shows
  2. Standing orders"A sourdough every Saturday" for regulars
  3. Wholesale portalNext-day orders for local cafés
